# LEVEL 1 — BASICS (Easy)

## Q1. What is 12.5% of 640?

**Correct option: (c) 80**  
**Your answer: (c) — Correct**

### Working
12.5% = 12.5/100 = 1/8

12.5% of 640 = 640 × 1/8 = 640 ÷ 8 = **80**

**Short trick:** 12.5% is one-eighth. Divide the number by 8.

## Q2. 18 is what percent of 72?

**Correct option: (b) 25%**  
**Your answer: (b) — Correct**

### Working
Percentage = (Part ÷ Total) × 100

= (18 ÷ 72) × 100 = 1/4 × 100 = **25%**

**Short trick:** 18 is one-quarter of 72, so the answer is 25%.

## Q3. If 35% of a number is 84, what is the number?

**Correct option: (d) 240**  
**Your answer: (d) — Correct**

### Working
Let the number be x.

35% of x = 84

(35/100) × x = 84

x = (84 × 100) ÷ 35 = **240**

**Short trick:** To find the whole when a percentage part is known, use  
Whole = (Given value × 100) ÷ Percentage.

## Q4. 16% of 25 + 25% of 16 = ?

**Correct option: (a) 8**  
**Your answer: (a) — Correct**

### Working
16% of 25 = (16/100) × 25 = 4

25% of 16 = (25/100) × 16 = 4

Total = 4 + 4 = **8**

## Q5. The price of a book rises from ₹80 to ₹100. What is the percentage increase?

**Correct option: (b) 25%**  
**Your answer: (b) — Correct**

### Working
Increase = ₹100 − ₹80 = ₹20

Percentage increase = (Increase ÷ Original price) × 100

= (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = **25%**

**Remember:** Percentage increase/decrease is calculated using the **original value** as the denominator.

# LEVEL 2 — COMPARISONS & SUCCESSIVE CHANGES

## Q6. A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what percent is B's salary less than A's?

**Correct option: (a) 20%**  
**Your answer: (a) — Correct**

### Working
Assume B's salary = ₹100.

A's salary = ₹100 + 25% of ₹100 = ₹125.

Difference = ₹125 − ₹100 = ₹25.

Percentage by which B is less than A:

= (25 ÷ 125) × 100 = **20%**

**Short trick:** If A is x% more than B, then B is less than A by  
[x ÷ (100 + x)] × 100%.

Here: 25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20%.

## Q7. The price of an item is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. What is the net change?

**Correct option: (c) 4% decrease**  
**Your answer: (b) — Incorrect**

### Working
Assume original price = ₹100.

After 20% increase: ₹100 × 1.20 = ₹120.

After 20% decrease: ₹120 × 0.80 = ₹96.

Net change = ₹96 − ₹100 = −₹4, so the price decreases by **4%**.

### 🔴 Weak topic: Successive percentage changes
**Short trick:** For two successive changes of +a% and −b%, net change is  
a − b − (ab/100) when the first change is an increase and the second a decrease.

For +20% and −20%: 20 − 20 − (20×20/100) = **−4%**.

**Remember:** Equal percentage increase and decrease do **not** cancel out, because the second percentage is calculated on the changed value.

## Q8. Two successive discounts of 10% and 20% equal a single discount of:

**Correct option: (d) 28%**  
**Your answer: (a) — Incorrect**

### Working
Assume marked price = ₹100.

After 10% discount: ₹100 × 0.90 = ₹90.

After 20% discount: ₹90 × 0.80 = ₹72.

Total discount = ₹100 − ₹72 = ₹28 = **28%** of the original price.

### 🔴 Weak topic: Successive discounts
**Short trick:** Equivalent discount for discounts a% and b%:

**a + b − (ab/100)%**

= 10 + 20 − (10×20/100)

= 30 − 2 = **28%**.

Do not simply add successive discounts.

## Q9. Sugar price rises by 25%. By what percent must consumption be reduced to keep expenditure unchanged?

**Correct option: (c) 20%**  
**Your answer: Not attempted**

### Working
Assume original price = ₹100 per unit and consumption = 100 units.

Original expenditure = ₹100 × 100 = ₹10,000.

New price after 25% increase = ₹125 per unit.

Required consumption = ₹10,000 ÷ ₹125 = 80 units.

Reduction = 100 − 80 = 20 units.

Percentage reduction = (20 ÷ 100) × 100 = **20%**.

### 🟠 Topic to practise: Price and consumption
**Short trick:** If price rises by x%, the required reduction in consumption to keep spending the same is:

**[x ÷ (100 + x)] × 100%**

For x = 25: (25 ÷ 125) × 100 = **20%**.

## Q10. Length increases by 20% and breadth decreases by 10%. What is the change in area?

**Correct option: (d) 8% increase**  
**Your answer: (a) — Incorrect**

### Working
Assume original length = 100 and breadth = 100.

Original area = 100 × 100 = 10,000.

New length = 100 × 1.20 = 120.

New breadth = 100 × 0.90 = 90.

New area = 120 × 90 = 10,800.

Percentage change = [(10,800 − 10,000) ÷ 10,000] × 100 = **8% increase**.

### 🔴 Weak topic: Percentage change in area
**Short trick:** For two dimensions changing by a% and b%, net area change is:

**a + b + (ab/100)%**

Use a negative sign for a decrease.

Here: 20 + (−10) + [20×(−10)/100]  
= 20 − 10 − 2 = **8% increase**.

# LEVEL 3 — APPLICATIONS (Medium)

## Q11. Population is 20,000. It increases by 10% in year one and 20% in year two. Find the population after two years.

**Correct option: (a) 26,400**  
**Your answer: (b) — Incorrect**

### Working
Initial population = 20,000.

After year one = 20,000 × 1.10 = 22,000.

After year two = 22,000 × 1.20 = **26,400**.

### 🟠 Topic to strengthen: Successive growth
**Short trick:** Multiply by a growth factor for each year:

Final value = Initial value × (1 + rate₁/100) × (1 + rate₂/100).

Here: 20,000 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 26,400.

Do not add 10% and 20% and apply 30% to the original population; the second year's increase is calculated on the updated population.

## Q12. In an election, 10% of votes are invalid. The winner gets 60% of valid votes and wins by 1,080 votes. Find total votes cast.

**Correct option: (b) 6,000**  
**Your answer: Not attempted**

### Working
Let valid votes = V.

Winner gets 60% of V, and the other candidate gets 40% of V.

Winning margin = 60% − 40% = 20% of V.

20% of V = 1,080.

V = 1,080 × 100 ÷ 20 = 5,400 valid votes.

Valid votes are 90% of total votes T because 10% are invalid.

0.90T = 5,400.

T = 5,400 ÷ 0.90 = **6,000 votes**.

### 🟠 Topic to practise: Election percentages
**Short trick:** First use the **winning margin** to find valid votes; then account for invalid votes.

1. Margin percentage = winner's percentage − loser's percentage.
2. Valid votes = margin × 100 ÷ margin percentage.
3. Total votes = valid votes × 100 ÷ valid-vote percentage.

## Q13. A student needs 40% marks to pass. He gets 178 and fails by 22 marks. Find maximum marks.

**Correct option: (c) 500**  
**Your answer: (c) — Correct**

### Working
Passing marks = 178 + 22 = 200.

200 is 40% of maximum marks M.

(40/100) × M = 200.

M = 200 × 100 ÷ 40 = **500 marks**.

**Short trick:** Maximum marks = Passing marks × 100 ÷ Passing percentage.

## Q14. Riya spends 30% of her salary on rent and 25% of the remaining amount on food. She saves ₹15,750. Find her salary.

**Correct option: (d) ₹30,000**  
**Your answer: (d) — Correct**

### Working
Assume salary = ₹x.

After spending 30% on rent, 70% remains.

Food costs 25% of the remaining 70%:

25% × 70% = 17.5% of salary.

Savings = 70% − 17.5% = 52.5% of salary.

52.5% of x = ₹15,750.

x = 15,750 × 100 ÷ 52.5 = **₹30,000**.

**Short trick:** When a percentage is spent from the *remaining amount*, calculate it from that remaining amount—not from the original salary.

## Q15. A machine worth ₹50,000 depreciates by 10% every year. Find its value after two years.

**Correct option: (b) ₹40,500**  
**Your answer: (b) — Correct**

### Working
After year one: ₹50,000 × 0.90 = ₹45,000.

After year two: ₹45,000 × 0.90 = **₹40,500**.

Shortcut formula:

Value after n years = Initial value × (1 − rate/100)ⁿ

= 50,000 × (0.90)² = ₹40,500.

**Short trick:** For depreciation, multiply by the remaining percentage each year. A 10% decrease means multiplying by 0.90.
